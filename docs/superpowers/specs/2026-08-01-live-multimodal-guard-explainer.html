The appliance on-site (Rioc)

The on-site hub

A small service at the location holds the camera, microphone and speaker. It streams what it sees and hears up to the AI, plays the AI's voice back out, and — crucially — decides locally and instantly when the person starts talking, so interrupting the guard feels immediate even though the AI lives far away.

camera + mic → AI · AI voice → speaker · "they're talking" → stop now
↓ ↑continuous video + audio over the internet
The AI brain (central, in the cloud)

One live AI session

A single MiniCPM-o model session stays "on the line" for the whole conversation — continuously watching the video and hearing the audio together, and speaking back in real time. This is the leap: not one photo per reply, but a running stream it reasons over.

The doorman

The shared AI handles many live conversations at once — as many as it can keep genuinely real-time across every location. A traffic director sends each new incident to the least-busy GPU, and capacity grows simply by adding more GPUs to the shared pool. A new incident is only ever turned away when the whole pool is genuinely full — so a rare simultaneous burst never degrades the conversations already in progress.

The record

Every conversation is saved — what the guard said live, and what the person said (transcribed just afterwards for the log).

Why it feels real

The AI is far away in the cloud, yet the conversation feels immediate and lifelike. Three choices make that true.

It sees and hears together

One model takes in the live video and the live audio as a single stream — so the guard reacts to what's actually happening, not to a single frozen photo.

Interrupting is instant

The on-site hub hears the person begin to speak and silences the guard immediately — it doesn't wait for a round-trip to the cloud. The AI catches up a moment later; the person never notices the distance.

It never fails quietly

If the AI is busy or the link drops, the operator sees it plainly on screen. No pretending a guard is on duty when it isn't.
